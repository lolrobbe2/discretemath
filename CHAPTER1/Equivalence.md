# Logical Equivalence

Two compound propositions `a(p, q, …)` and `b(p, q, …)` are **logically equivalent** if the statement

`a(p, q, …) ⇔ b(p, q, …)`

is a **[tautology](#tautology)**.  
Equivalently, `a` and `b` always have the same truth value for any given values of  
`p`, `q`, …

`Notation:`
- `a(p, q, …) ≡ b(p, q, …)`

**Example:**  
- `¬(p ⇔ q) ≡ p ⇔ ¬q`

---

## Tautology (TRUE) <a name="tautology"></a>
A **[tautology](#tautology)** is a proposition that is always *true*, regardless of the values of its logical variables.

**Example:**  
- `p ∨ ¬p`

## Contradiction (FALSE) <a name="contradiction"></a>
A **[contradiction](#contradiction)** is a proposition that is always *false*, regardless of the values of its logical variables,  
opposite of a [tautology](#tautology).

**Example:**  
- `p ∧ ¬p`

## Contingency (Eventuality) <a name="contingency"></a>
A **[contingency](#contingency)** is a proposition that is neither a [tautology](#tautology) nor a [contradiction](#contradiction).

**Example:**  
- `¬p`
