# Mathematical Statements and Proofs

A mathematical theorem, corollary, property, … usually states a proposition of the form:

- `p ⇒ q` (or `p ⇔ q`)

Where:
- `p`: already known or proven properties, axioms, assumptions, …
- `q`: the statement or claim that must be proven

A **mathematical proof** must then demonstrate (prove) that the stated proposition is true.

---

## Example

For the implication `p ⇒ q` to be valid, it must hold that **if `p` is true, then `q` is also true**.  
If `p` is false, the truth value of `q` does not matter.

---

## Proof Strategies

There are several basic strategies to structure a mathematical proof.  
Nevertheless, proving statements remains a largely **creative activity**, based on insight and experience.
