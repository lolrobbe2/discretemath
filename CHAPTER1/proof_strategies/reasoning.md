# Reasoning

## Deduction vs Induction

### Deductive Reasoning
In `deductive reasoning`, one starts from a general statement and derives a more specific conclusion.

`Examples:`
- From “It is always warm at the seaside” follows  
  “It will be warm at the seaside tomorrow”
- From “Everyone born in France has French nationality” follows  
  “Anne-Marie, who was born in Lyon, has French nationality”

---

### Inductive Reasoning
In `inductive reasoning`, one starts from one or more specific cases and draws a general conclusion.

`Examples:`
- Experiments in physics, chemistry, … lead to general *laws*
- “All swans are white” based on multiple observations of swans

---

## Mathematical Perspective

Strictly speaking, only `deductive reasoning` is mathematically correct:  
starting from generally accepted facts, one tries to prove new, universally valid properties.

However, `inductive reasoning` is often used to initially *formulate* or *conjecture* a theorem.
